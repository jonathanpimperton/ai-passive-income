## Your monthly payment is £1,842 — and you pay £252,600 in interest

## UK mortgage basics: repayment vs interest-only

Most UK mortgages are repayment (capital + interest), meaning each monthly payment reduces the balance. The alternative — interest-only — charges just the interest each month (£1,375 on £300K at 5.5%) but leaves the full £300,000 due at the end of the term.

Interest-only mortgages were common before 2008 but lenders now require a credible repayment strategy (usually investments or property sale). For this scenario, we use a standard repayment mortgage.

## Overpayments: the UK advantage

Most UK lenders allow overpayments of up to 10% of the outstanding balance per year without early repayment charges. On a £300,000 mortgage, that is up to £30,000/year in extra payments.

| Overpayment | Payoff time | Interest saved |
|-------------|------------|----------------|
| £100/month | 22 years 3 months | £33,800 |
| £200/month | 20 years | £59,100 |
| £500/month | 16 years 2 months | £108,300 |
| 10% lump sum in year 1 | 22 years 6 months | ~£38,000 |

£200/month in overpayments cuts 5 years off the mortgage and saves nearly £60,000. This is often the best risk-free "return" available — equivalent to earning 5.5% tax-free on the overpaid amount.

## Fixed vs variable: the UK decision

UK mortgages typically offer 2-year or 5-year fixed periods before reverting to the lender's standard variable rate (SVR), which is usually 2–3% above the fixed rate.

- **2-year fix at 5.0%:** Lower rate now, but you remortgage sooner (with fees of £1,000–£2,000 each time).
- **5-year fix at 5.5%:** Slightly higher rate, but payment certainty for 5 years and fewer remortgaging fees.
- **Tracker (base rate + margin):** Moves with the Bank of England rate. Good if you expect rates to fall.

On a £300,000 mortgage, the 0.5% difference between a 2-year and 5-year fix is about £88/month. Whether to lock in depends on your view of where rates are heading.
