## What $10,000 covers

$10,000 is a common starter emergency fund target. It helps with:

- **Car repair or replacement:** A major repair or a replacement car can cost thousands of dollars.
- **Medical emergency:** With insurance you still owe deductibles and copays, up to your plan's out-of-pocket maximum for the year.
- **Job loss bridge:** $10,000 covers about {{ 10000 / 4500 | num(1) }} months of the $4,500 monthly essentials used in our [6-month emergency fund example](/scenarios/6-month-emergency-fund/).
- **Home repair:** A failed water heater, furnace or plumbing emergency usually costs hundreds to a couple of thousand dollars.
- **Unexpected travel:** A family emergency that needs last-minute flights.

Without savings, these events go on credit cards at 20%+ APR, creating a debt spiral that costs far more than the original emergency.

## Ways to find {{ savingsMonthly(10000, 500, 4.5, 12) | usd }}/month

Some of these are one-time boosts, others are recurring savings:

1. **Cancel unused subscriptions.** Audit every card and bank statement for the last three months.
2. **Cook at home.** Replacing restaurant meals and delivery with home cooking is usually the biggest single cut.
3. **Reduce grocery waste.** Meal planning and using leftovers cut the weekly shop.
4. **Switch phone plans.** Prepaid or MVNO plans often cost a fraction of the major carriers' postpaid plans.
5. **Sell unused items.** A one-time boost from electronics, furniture and clothing.
6. **Side gig income.** Freelancing, tutoring or delivery, part-time.
7. **Negotiate bills.** Call insurance, internet and phone providers and ask for their retention offer.
8. **Use cashback on regular purchases.** Small, but it adds up on groceries and fuel.
9. **Pause savings for non-essential goals.** Temporarily redirect the vacation or hobby fund.
10. **Reduce energy costs.** A programmable thermostat, LED bulbs and off-peak usage.
11. **Tax refund.** A $3,000 refund, for example, covers {{ 3000 / savingsMonthly(10000, 500, 4.5, 12) | num(1) }} months of saving in one deposit.
12. **Employer HSA/FSA.** Pre-tax health savings reduce taxable income, potentially freeing up cash.

You do not need all 12; any combination adding up to {{ savingsMonthly(10000, 500, 4.5, 12) | usd }}/month works.
