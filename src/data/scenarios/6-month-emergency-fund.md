**At $4,500 per month in essential expenses, a 6-month emergency fund requires {{ 4500 * 6 | usd }}.** This covers rent or mortgage, utilities, food, insurance, transportation, and minimum debt payments for half a year without any income. The same fund for 3 months is {{ 4500 * 3 | usd }}, and for 12 months {{ 4500 * 12 | usd }}.

## Why 6 months is the standard recommendation

Financial advisers commonly recommend 3–6 months of expenses, and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says the right amount depends on your situation ([CFPB: an essential guide to building an emergency fund](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/)). The range depends on how exposed your income is:

**3 months is enough if:**
- You have a stable job with low layoff risk
- You are part of a dual-income household
- You have no dependents
- You have access to other backup resources

**6 months is better if:**
- You are the sole earner in your household
- You work in a cyclical industry (tech, construction, finance)
- You have children or other dependents
- You are self-employed or have irregular income
- You have a medical condition that could affect your ability to work

**9–12 months makes sense if:**
- Your job search could take a long time (senior or specialized roles)
- Your income is entirely commission-based
- You are planning a career change

## Where to keep your emergency fund

An emergency fund needs to be instantly accessible, not invested in the stock market. Appropriate places:

- **High-yield savings account:** The best option for most people. The balance is FDIC-insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/deposit-insurance)) and available within 1-2 business days. The rate is variable and follows the Federal Reserve's target range, which stood at 3.75-4.00% after the 17 September 2026 decision ([Federal Reserve, read 15 November 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/openmarket.htm)). Savings rates differ by bank, so check what you can actually get. This page assumes 4.5% a year as an example, not a quote: on {{ 4500 * 6 | usd }} that is about {{ 4500 * 6 * 4.5 / 100 | usd }} of interest a year.
- **Money market account:** Similar rates to high-yield savings, sometimes with check-writing ability.
- **Short-term Treasury bills:** Their yields follow short-term interest rates and the interest is exempt from state and local tax. Less liquid, so good for the portion you are unlikely to need within 30 days.

Avoid CDs with early withdrawal penalties, brokerage accounts with market risk, or anything that takes more than 3 days to access.

Faster strategies:
- **Tax refund:** If you get one, deposit it straight into emergency savings. A $2,500 refund alone is {{ 2500 / 500 | int }} months of saving at $500.
- **Automate first:** Set up a direct transfer on payday so the money never hits your spending account.
- **Start with $1,000:** A mini emergency fund covers most car repairs and medical copays. Build from there.
- **Side income:** Dedicate all freelance or side gig income to the fund until it is full.
