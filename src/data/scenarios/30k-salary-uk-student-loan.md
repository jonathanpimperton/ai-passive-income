## You take home £24,876 — about £2,073 per month

**On a £30,000 salary with a Plan 2 student loan, your take-home pay is approximately £24,876 per year after income tax, National Insurance, and loan repayments.** That is roughly £2,073 per month. Without the student loan, you would take home £25,120 — the loan costs you £244 per year or about £20 per month.

## Full deduction breakdown

**Income tax: £3,486**

- Taxable income: £30,000 − £12,570 = £17,430

**National Insurance: £1,394**

**Student loan (Plan 2): £244**

- Repayable income: £30,000 − £27,295 = £2,705

## How Plan 2 repayments actually work

Student loan repayments in the UK are not like a standard debt where you choose how much to pay. The rules are fixed:

- **Threshold:** You only repay when earning above £27,295 per year (2025/26 rate)
- **Rate:** 9% of everything above the threshold
- **Collection:** Deducted automatically through PAYE, like tax
- **Write-off:** Any remaining balance is wiped after 30 years from the April after you graduated

At £30,000, you are only £2,705 above the threshold, so repayments are minimal. Your employer deducts about £20 per month. If your salary drops below £27,295, repayments stop automatically.

## What this means for your actual debt

£244 per year in repayments barely touches the interest being added to most Plan 2 balances. The interest rate on Plan 2 loans is RPI plus up to 3%, depending on your income. At £30,000, the rate is RPI (which has been 3–10% in recent years).

If you owe £40,000 and interest adds £2,000–£3,000 per year, your £244 repayment means the balance is growing. This is normal and by design — the system functions more like a graduate tax than a traditional loan. For most Plan 2 borrowers, the balance will be written off before it is fully repaid.

Whether to make voluntary overpayments depends on your balance and expected career earnings. If you expect to earn well above £50,000 for most of your career, you will likely repay in full and overpaying saves interest. If your career trajectory is more modest, the write-off will clear the remaining balance and overpayments would be money wasted.

## Salary milestones and repayment amounts

- **£27,295 or below:** No repayment
- **£30,000:** £244/year (£20/month)
- **£35,000:** £694/year (£58/month)
- **£40,000:** £1,143/year (£95/month)
- **£50,000:** £2,043/year (£170/month)

Each £1,000 pay rise above the threshold adds £90/year (£7.50/month) in student loan repayments on top of the 28% income tax and NI you already pay. The effective marginal rate for someone with a Plan 2 loan earning between £27,295 and £50,270 is 37% (20% tax + 8% NI + 9% student loan).

## Plan 1 vs Plan 2 comparison

If you started university before September 2012, you are on Plan 1, which has a lower threshold of £24,990 but the same 9% rate. On £30,000, Plan 1 repayments would be £451/year — nearly double the Plan 2 amount because of the lower threshold.
