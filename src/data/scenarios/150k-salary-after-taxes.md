## You take home roughly $113,791 in a no-income-tax state

**On a $150,000 salary filing single in a state with no income tax (like Texas, Florida, or Washington), you keep approximately $113,791 after federal income tax and FICA.** That is $9,483 per month. In a state with income tax, you keep less — the exact amount depends on where you live.

## Federal tax breakdown

**Gross salary:** $150,000
**Standard deduction (2026):** $16,100
**Taxable income:** $133,900

Federal income tax (2026 brackets, single):
- 10% on first $12,400 = $1,240
- 12% on $12,401–$50,400 = $4,560
- 22% on $50,401–$105,700 = $12,166
- 24% on $105,701–$133,900 = $6,768

**Total federal income tax: $24,734**

FICA taxes (on full $150,000):
- Social Security (6.2% up to $184,500): $9,300
- Medicare (1.45%): $2,175

**Total FICA: $11,475**

**Total deductions: $36,209**
**Take-home: $113,791**

Your effective federal tax rate is 16.5%, and your effective total rate (including FICA) is 24.1%.

Living in California versus Texas costs you roughly $10,100/year in state income tax — $842/month. New York City residents face both state and city income tax, losing $12,800/year more than someone in Florida doing the same job.

## $150K with a 401(k) contribution

Contributing 15% to a traditional 401(k) ($22,500/year, just under the $24,500 limit):

- Taxable income drops to $111,400
- Federal income tax falls to $19,334 (saving $5,400)
- FICA stays the same ($11,475 — 401k does not reduce FICA)
- Take-home: $150,000 − $19,334 − $11,475 − $22,500 = $96,691

You receive $17,100 less in cash, but $22,500 went into your retirement account — and you saved $5,400 in taxes. The effective cost of the $22,500 contribution is only $17,100.

## Marginal vs effective rate

At $150,000, your marginal federal rate is 24%. This means every additional dollar (from a raise, bonus, or side income) is taxed at 24% federal + FICA + state. But your effective rate on the full salary is only 16.5%.

Understanding the difference matters for decisions like:
- **Overtime or bonus:** Taxed at the marginal rate, not the effective rate. A $10,000 bonus in a no-tax state yields about $6,835 after federal tax and FICA.
- **Roth vs traditional 401(k):** If you expect to be in a lower bracket in retirement, traditional (pre-tax) saves more now. If you expect the same or higher, Roth (post-tax) avoids higher future rates.
- **Side income:** Freelance income on top of $150K is taxed at 24% federal + 15.3% self-employment tax (both sides of FICA). The combined marginal rate can reach 39%+ even in a no-tax state.

## What $150K buys in different cities

Take-home is only part of the equation. Cost of living varies dramatically:

- **Austin, TX:** $9,483/month take-home, average rent $1,700 for 1BR. Very comfortable.
- **Denver, CO:** $8,988/month, average rent $1,800. Comfortable with room to save.
- **New York City:** $8,416/month, average rent $3,500 for 1BR in Manhattan. Tight after housing.
- **San Francisco:** $8,641/month, average rent $3,200. Similar to NYC.

A $150K salary in Austin or Nashville provides a higher standard of living than $150K in San Francisco or New York, despite being the same gross number.
